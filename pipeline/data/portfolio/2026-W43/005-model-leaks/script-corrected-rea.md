# 005 model leaks — CORRECTED script (REA correction, 2026-10-26)

## What was wrong in the original script
- Original treated "Claude REA banned" as a search for a model called Claude REA that could not be found, and collapsed it into Anthropic's policy/account bans.
- Correction: REA is not a model at all. REA stands for Reverse Engineer Anything. It is a GitHub repo, github.com/morluto/rea, an MIT-licensed MCP server and CLI that lets a coding agent use reverse-engineering tools to inspect software you own or are authorized to examine. It is not a Claude model, not an Anthropic product, and no Anthropic ban of REA was found. The Anthropic bans are real but separate.
- Date correction in the shipped list: Claude Opus 5.5 was released September 22, 2026, and Gemini 4 Argon was announced September 30, 2026 and generally available October 13, 2026. Neither was new in the week of October 25, 2026. The original script's "in its limited form" for Argon understated this; the corrected script gives the dates.
- This corrected script supersedes vo2/script.txt for any future render. v10 and v11 were built from the uncorrected script and are outdated on the REA section and the shipped-list dates.

## Corrected voiceover script (full text, in order)

Narrator: One video title this week promised four huge model updates. GPT-7, Claude REA banned, Gemini Carbon insane, Qwen 4 leaks. I checked all four against what the companies actually said. One is a rumor, one is a repo mistaken for a model, one is a leak with no product, and one is real but not shipped. Here is the scorecard, so you do not build on a rumor.

Narrator: Before the evidence, here is the thought I want you to carry through all four names. A rumor is not just a bad fact. It is a mirror. It shows you what you already wanted to be true. When a title promises the next giant model, the next ban, the next leak, it is not only selling news, it is selling certainty in a field that has almost none. The directive is simple, and it is hard: want the truth more than you want the headline. If you can hold that for six minutes, the rest of this video will feel less like a debunk and more like a discipline.

Narrator: First, GPT-7 Bel. The whole thing traces to one anonymous post on August 25th. No document, no named source, and it did not even say GPT-7 — that label came later. OpenAI has never used the name Bel in public, and on October 11th its own models page listed GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol, and GPT-6 Luna. No Bel, no GPT-7. What OpenAI did say, on September 8th, is that it has an internal model significantly more capable than GPT-6 Astra. That is true, and it is not the same as saying that model is Bel, or that it is GPT-7. Verdict: unverified.

Narrator: Second, REA. And this is the trick inside the trick: REA is not a model, and it is not a Claude product at all. REA stands for Reverse Engineer Anything. It is a GitHub repo, github.com/morluto/rea, a free, open-source tool that lets a coding agent use reverse-engineering tools to inspect software you own or are allowed to examine — binaries, apps, websites — and explain how it works, down to the code underneath. It plugs into agents like Claude Code, Codex, and Cursor through an MCP server, and the analysis runs locally. It went viral in mid-October, and that virality is exactly why it got pasted into a model-news title. It was never banned by Anthropic, because Anthropic does not own it, and it is not a model to ban.

Narrator: What is real, separately, is two Anthropic bans, and neither is REA. On October 8th, Anthropic updated its usage policy, effective November 12th, to ban sustained, needless cruelty toward Claude — and the main enforcement is Claude ending the conversation, not a model being pulled. And on October 12th, Anthropic said it banned tens of thousands of accounts for trying to copy Claude by distillation. So banned is real, but it is accounts and a policy, and REA is a repo. Three different things, one scary title.

Narrator: Pause on that word, banned, because it is doing philosophical work here, not just legal work. To ban an account is to draw a boundary around a community. To ban a behavior is to say, this is not how we will treat a mind, even a simulated one. To ban a model would be something else entirely: to erase a capability from the world. And to mistake a repo for a model is a fourth collapse: to mistake the workshop for the worker. The title collapses all of them into one scary word, and that collapse is the trick. When you hear a word that big, ask what exactly was banned, by whom, and from whom — and whether the thing named is even a model at all. Precision is not pedantry here. Precision is the difference between fear and understanding.

Narrator: Third, Gemini 4 Carbon. This one is the closest to real, and it is still not a product. Business Insider reported on October 9th that Google staff were testing Carbon inside Google, on its internal coding platform, and one employee said it feels like Opus 5.5 for coding. Feels like is not a benchmark, there is no model card, no price, no release date, and Google has not confirmed it. A checkpoint briefly showed up on LMArena around October 23rd and was pulled. So Carbon is a leak about an internal checkpoint, not a model you can use today.

Narrator: Fourth, Qwen 4. Alibaba said on September 22nd that Qwen 4 is in training. It named tiers — Max, Plus, Flash, and a 27B model — but gave no specs, no date, and as of October 24th there were no Qwen 4 weights on Hugging Face. The thing you can actually look at today is Qwen 3.8 Flash-Next, which Alibaba itself called a preview of the architecture that will underpin Qwen 4. So Qwen 4 is real as a project, not shipped as a model. And Qwen 4 Coder does not exist yet.

Narrator: A leak is a model before it has a name, a price, or a promise. It lives in the gap between what a lab knows and what the public is allowed to know, and that gap is where imagination rushes in. That is not a flaw in you. It is how humans think about power we cannot yet touch. We fill the unknown with the biggest thing we can picture. The discipline is to let the unknown stay unknown for a little longer. A checkpoint is not a product. A feeling is not a benchmark. A training run is not a release. Naming each stage honestly is how you keep your own thinking clean.

Narrator: So if you are picking a model this week, pick from the ones that actually shipped: GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5 — released September 22nd, not this week — Gemini 4 Argon — announced September 30th, generally available October 13th — and Qwen 3.8. The other four names are a rumor, a repo, a leak, and a training run. That is not less interesting — it is just the difference between news and hype, and that difference is the whole point.

Narrator: One last test before you share anything this week. Ask who benefits if you believe it now, before it is confirmed. If the answer is a channel selling urgency, a seller selling access, or your own desire to be first, slow down. Speed is not insight. Being first to repeat a rumor is not the same as being right, and in a field that moves this fast, the person who waits for confirmation is the one still standing on solid ground when the hype cycle turns.

Narrator: And here is the directive I want you to leave with. Build on what shipped. Watch what is rumored, but do not bet your work, your money, or your reputation on it. Keep a ledger, even a mental one: claimed, sourced, confirmed, shipped. Four columns. Most of the internet lives in the first two. Your advantage is living in the last two. That is not cynicism. That is stewardship. The future will arrive whether you hype it or not, and when it does, you will be ready for it precisely because you did not pretend it was already here.
