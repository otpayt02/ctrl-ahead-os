# First-party upload analytics: pre-build check

Date: 2026-10-18. Scope: local tracking for Oliver's own YouTube uploads. This pass checks the existing [Shorts retention research](shorts-retention-research.md) before implementation; it does not certify its uncited benchmark ranges.

## Six lanes

1. **Official / first-party — 4 sources checked; high confidence; CHANGE.** [YouTube Studio Shorts analytics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217) defines Shown in feed and How many chose to view. [YouTube retention help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415) places first-30-second retention at the video level. [YouTube CTR FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154) says half of channels/videos fall between 2% and 10% CTR, with variation by traffic source. [YouTube A/B help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400) says native tests are unavailable for Shorts and a conclusive result can take days or up to two weeks. Build owner-entered checkpoints; distinguish an early review from a final A/B result.
2. **Peer-reviewed / research — 3 searches, 2 relevant papers checked; medium confidence; CONFIRM.** [Cakmak et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04605) study 685,842 Shorts in selected domains and report an entertainment/positive-content drift; this does not establish a universal 15–30-second optimum. [Rajendran et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18208) study 250 creators' short/long relationship, with limits for individual channel prediction. Keep format experiments first-party. No peer-reviewed source in this pass validates the proposed exact 70% viewed or 60–75% first-30-second thresholds.
3. **Industry benchmarks — 1 official platform benchmark plus the supplied research dump checked; medium confidence; CHANGE.** YouTube's official CTR FAQ gives a broad 2–10% range, not a 3–6% rule. Treat 3–6% CTR, 60–75% first-30-second retention, and 70% viewed as Oliver's starting review targets, never algorithm gates.
4. **Community / creator data — 2 creator reports checked; low confidence; IGNORE for thresholds.** [This creator report](https://www.reddit.com/r/NewTubers/comments/1ug71np/here_are_my_anonymous_youtube_stats_after_52/) and [this retention anecdote](https://www.reddit.com/r/NewTubers/comments/1rlkyd5/analyzed_my_last_120_videos_to_find_what_the_algorithm_actually_rewards/) are useful examples but cannot determine universal targets. Let Oliver's own upload cohorts replace them over time.
5. **Open datasets / leaks — 2 public dataset/repository leads checked; low confidence; CONFIRM owner boundary.** [This public Shorts study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04605) uses observable content/recommendations; [this community repository](https://github.com/datastudy-nl/Youtube-Shorts-Algorithm) asserts aggregate behavioral rules without access to Oliver's Studio. Neither provides verified owner-only feed or retention measurements for his uploads. No private data was accessed.
6. **Contradiction check — 3 plan claims checked; high confidence for platform limits; CHANGE.** The supplied dump's threshold language exceeds official support; native A/B tests do not apply to Shorts; and a 24–48-hour review is too early to declare a native A/B winner. Keep the thresholds labeled experimental, schedule early checks, and record the platform's eventual test result separately.

## Verdict and plan delta

**GO** for a local SQLite/CSV loop, with four changes: (1) manual Studio entry without assuming OAuth; (2) explicit 24h, 48h, and 7d checkpoints with missing data preserved as unknown; (3) format-specific fields and 100-minus-viewed swipe calculation; (4) long-form A/B roadmap wording that separates early review from final results. Prompt guidance may use the supplied creative tactics but must not describe them as proven platform rules.
